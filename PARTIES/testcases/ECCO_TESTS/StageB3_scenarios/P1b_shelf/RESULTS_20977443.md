# P1b results — released fine-sand grain beneath an Antarctic ice-shelf base

**Analysed 2026-10-01.** The design, pre-flights and decisions are in
[YANG_ECCO_B3_P1b_SHELF.md](../../../../../docs/md_files/NASA-JPL/YANG_ECCO_B3_P1b_SHELF.md).
This file reports the completed production segment.

**Headline.** A single 0.2 mm quartz grain released into the meltwater sublayer under the
ice settled at a steady 14.4 mm/s and landed after 0.28 s. On the way it dragged about
**one grain volume of pure-meltwater equivalent** out of the sublayer. It left a persistent
meltwater trail down to the bottom, centred about 9 grain diameters below the ice.

All runs closed their budgets to roundoff. The near-ice layer (≲ 3.4 d) carries a measured
band-kernel bias and is flagged, not interpreted.

## 1. Grain settling ([F1](results_20977443/F1_grain.png))

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Start | at rest, centre 0.85 d below the ice interface |
| Terminal velocity (t > 4) | **−1.965 U_ref = 14.35 mm/s** = 0.920 × Schiller–Naumann w_s |
| Lateral drift | ≤ 5.5×10⁻⁵ d (the grain falls exactly on axis) |
| 90% of terminal reached | t = 1.30, at 2.65 d below the ice |
| Deceleration begins | bottom gap 1.38 d |
| Maximum deceleration | bottom gap 0.081 d (contact model engages) |
| Contact (gap < 0.05 d) | t = 10.35 (0.283 s); no rebound |
| Final state | gap 0.004 d, v = −0.0085 (still creeping in the lubrication film) |

Speed as a fraction of terminal, by centre depth below the ice:

| Depth | 1 d | 2 d | 3 d | 4 d | 6 d | 8 d |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v / v_terminal | 0.53 | 0.83 | 0.92 | 0.96 | 0.99 | 1.00 |

- **Why terminal speed is 8% below the estimate:** the Schiller–Naumann estimate is for an
  isolated grain. The 8% deficit is consistent with hindrance from the 8 d periodic
  neighbours. This is not a confinement-corrected terminal velocity.
- **Slow approach to terminal:** the grain's inertial response time is 0.14 code units,
  yet it needs about 3 d to approach terminal speed. Two causes combine and cannot be
  separated here:
  - the no-slip ice ceiling, which hinders a sphere moving away from a wall over several
    diameters at Re ≈ 2;
  - the meltwater stratification.

  Separating them would need a no-ice twin.

## 2. Meltwater carried down by the grain — headline result ([F2](results_20977443/F2_meltwater_excess.png), [F3](results_20977443/F3_tracer_slices.png))

The meltwater tracer integral below depth z, P1b minus the matched default control:
- the comparison uses identical numerics, so band-kernel bias is common-mode;
- results are in grain volumes (π/6 d³, i.e. 4.2×10⁻³ mm³ at d = 0.2 mm).

| Below depth | Peak excess | Excess at t = 18.3 |
|---|---|---|
| 2 d | 0.73 | 0.57 |
| 4 d | **1.26** (t ≈ 5.5) | **0.99** |
| 6 d | 0.85 | 0.78 |
| 8 d | 0.47 | 0.47 |
| 12 d | 0.22 | 0.22 |

- **Mechanism (F3):** the grain drags a filament of sublayer water (meltwater-rich) behind it.
- **Shallow part rebounds:** below 4–6 d the excess peaks around t ≈ 5–8 and then relaxes.
  The displaced light water partly rises back and spreads into a broad plume 3–8 d below the
  ice, consistent with restratification (buoyancy period ≈ 18 code units).
- **Deep part persists:** below 8 d and 12 d the excess steps up as the grain passes and then
  stays flat. The deep filament diffuses but does not return within the window.
- **Profile at t = 18.3:**
  - the excess is centred 9.4 d below the ice;
  - the matching deficit, −0.29 d³, sits in the top 2 d;
  - the net is +0.008 d³, equal to the small melt difference between the runs.
- **Effect on horizontally averaged stratification:** very small. In the liquid,
  |Δ⟨S⟩| ≤ 0.0029 (at 1.9 d) and |Δ⟨θ⟩| ≤ 0.0023 (at 3.8 d). The box holds one grain per
  64 d² of cross-section, about 0.8% by volume for a one-grain-thick debris sheet. So these
  are **per-grain** numbers, to be scaled by sediment flux and concentration, not read as
  bulk-mixing results.
- **Energy:** dissipation integrated over the run is 532 (code units) in P1b against
  1.5×10⁻¹⁴ in the quiescent control. It peaks at t = 9.3, during the approach to the bottom.

## 2b. Concentration dependence — `conc4`, job 21006061 ([F6](results_20977443/F6_concentration.png))

**Setup:**
- one grain per 4 × 4 d instead of 8 × 8 d, about 3.3% by volume for a one-grain-thick
  debris sheet against 0.8%;
- same physics, operator and build;
- t = 18.28 in 7 h 40 min, 982 SU;
- it ended at the checkpoint-stop with no failures.

Per grain, against the default control scaled per area (exact, since the control is
horizontally uniform):

| | 8 × 8 d (P1b) | 4 × 4 d (conc4) | conc4 / P1b |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terminal velocity | −1.965 | −1.618 | **0.82** |
| Contact time | 10.35 | 12.13 | — |
| Meltwater below 4 d at t = 17.5 (grain volumes) | 1.01 | 0.62 | **0.61** |
| Peak below 4 d | 1.26 | 0.67 | 0.53 |
| Below 8 d | 0.47 | 0.29 | 0.62 |
| Below 12 d | 0.22 | 0.16 | 0.74 |

Interpretation:
- Closer packing hinders settling by about 18%.
- It cuts the meltwater each grain carries by about 40%, more so in the shallow,
  rebounding part.
- Per unit area, 4× more grains carry only about 2.4× more meltwater below 4 d.
- **Grain-carried meltwater transport is therefore sub-linear in sediment concentration.**
  A flux estimate cannot be a single-grain value multiplied by the number of grains.
- The reservoir is not limiting: the sublayer holds far more meltwater per grain than is
  carried. The reduction is hydrodynamic: collective wakes and hindered settling.

## 3. Model limitations, measured ([F4](results_20977443/F4_operator_comparison.png), [F5](results_20977443/F5_net_melt.png))

**Band-kernel sign artifact (default operator).** Net melt is negative, i.e. net freezing,
in both default runs:

| Run | Net melt by t ≈ 10 | Net melt at end |
|---|---|---|
| P1b | −0.43 d³ | −0.34 d³ (t = 20.5) |
| Default control | −0.43 d³ | −0.37 d³ (t = 18.3) |
| Yang control | melts throughout | +0.34 d³ (t = 15.4) |

The default runs' net freezing reaches about −0.43 d³ near t ≈ 10, then slowly recovers.
Under the volume-averaged liquidus the initial band is undercooled. **Melt rate and melt sign
from these runs are not physical results.**

**Reach of the near-ice contamination** (default minus Yang control, no grain, deepest depth
where the difference exceeds 0.01):

| t | θ | S |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.81 d | 0.40 d |
| 3 | 1.48 d | 0.65 d |
| 7 | 2.31 d | 0.98 d |
| 12 | 2.98 d | 1.23 d |
| 15 | 3.35 d | 1.40 d |

It grows roughly as √t. The interface is pinned about 0.4 K too warm. The P1b-minus-control
signal in the shaded zone of F2b is common-mode-corrected, but it develops on a biased state.

**Wake ice (P1b only).** At most 0.0041 d³ of ice lies more than 0.5 d below the interface,
about 0.8% of a grain volume. It sits along the grain's path in the top ~1.5 d. The default
control has 3.8×10⁻⁵ d³. It is a band-kernel and wake interaction (compare the B.2 ice-tongue
note).

**Yang operator.** It is unstable with a moving resolved grain (design record §8.4), so it is
usable only as the no-grain reference.

## 5. Assessment against the P1b criteria (design record §6)

**Met:**
- conservation;
- numerical health;
- rigidity;
- the measured quantities: settling through and below the sublayer, grain-carried
  meltwater, wake and restratification, dissipation.

**Not met, documented:**
- the near-ice layer and the melt rate and its sign are contaminated by the default band
  kernel;
- the terminal velocity is box-hindered;
- the approach to terminal speed cannot separate ceiling hindrance from stratification.

**Not done:** P2 (32/d resolution comparison).

## 6. Suggested next steps (none submitted)

1. **Repair the Yang operator at resolved sediment.** For example, use capacity
   max(C_L, C_S)+δ in support cells. Then test it in a short moving-grain diagnostic. This
   is needed for any melt-rate or near-ice claim.
2. **Run a no-ice twin**: the same sublayer profiles with a no-slip lid, no VOF, and the
   same grain. It separates ceiling hindrance from stratification drag, cheaply, at a few
   hundred SU on a smaller box.
3. **Run P2** (32 cells/d). At P1b's measured throughput (7.4k cell-steps per core-second)
   it would cost about 9.4k SU to reach t = 20.5. It is worthwhile once (1) settles which
   operator to use.
4. ~~Run a scaled-concentration study~~ **done (§2b): the transport is sub-linear.** A
   third spacing (for example 6 d or 12 d) would fit the scaling.
