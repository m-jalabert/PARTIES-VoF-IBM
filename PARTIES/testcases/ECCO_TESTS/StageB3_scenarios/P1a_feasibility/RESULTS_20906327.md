**Net freezing, with simultaneous local melting.** The in-solver physical ice
volume rises monotonically over the 805 saved profile times, from
**31.4745276870 to 31.4808039071 d³**: a gain of **0.00627622009 d³**, or
**0.0199406%**. This corresponds to an area-averaged added ice depth of
0.000392264d (0.392264 μm for d=1 mm), not a uniform interface displacement.
The far-from-grain F=0.5 contour moves from y=8 to **7.99434814**; its interpolated
5–95% width changes from about **6.43 to 6.33 cells**. These are diffuse-interface
measurements, not a sharp-interface melt law or proof of scalar convergence.

Reconstructing the actual source formula from saved fields confirms both signs:
over the saved-field interval, trapezoidal integrals of positive and negative
sources are approximately **+0.06199795 and −0.06827073 d³**. These sampled
integrals are diagnostic, not the exact RK source history. The negative part
slightly outweighs the positive part. The initial scalar band itself already
contains superheated and undercooled portions relative to the selected liquidus.
Neither this short startup nor the earlier conduction estimate demonstrates
that later melt-out is impossible; neither supports a quantitative release-time
prediction. Extrapolating the observed shell fraction linearly to release would
be unjustified.

**Tracer interpretation.** The archived solver explicitly uses signed `m` in
both the phase change and tracer source (`VOF_DIFFUSE_compute_melt_rate` and
`Conc_add_meltwater_RHS`). When freezing withdraws tracer from initially zero
tracer, the diagnostic becomes negative. The observed physical range, excluding
sediment support, is **−0.01762843 to +0.01133145**. No saved bulk-liquid cell
(`C_L>0.95`, `C_S<0.05`) has tracer below −1e−10; negatives are confined to the
interface/ice region. This is consistent with the signed source and is not,
by itself, evidence of an advective oscillation. However, the tracer is **not
everywhere a nonnegative meltwater-origin fraction** in this freezing case.
Its mass balance passes; a water-origin or mixing interpretation needs a
separate explicitly conservative treatment of freezing, before any such claim.
Do not simply clip the negative values, which would break the checked balance.

**Rigidity interpretation.** The audit uses B.2's mask, actual ice fraction >0.95
and C_S<0.001, and the saved velocity-component speed convention. The largest
absolute bulk-ice speed is only **7.2643e−8 m/s** (about 4.50e−6 in code units).
Domain flow is also weak, so the relative ratio deserves that absolute context;
it nevertheless exceeds the predeclared 1% criterion persistently and cannot
be marked passed. The worst-ratio point is near y=8.145833, at the lower bulk-ice
edge. Do not silently weaken the criterion or change Darcy damping based on
this one case; diagnose projection/penalization/interface behavior first.

**Mask convention checked against source.** C_S is a shifted tanh whose 0.05
contour marks the physical grain surface; its integral is not geometric grain
volume. For ice totals, exclude C_S≥0.05, exactly as `ECCO_write_profiles` and
the ice transport do. Counting `max(1-C_S-C_L,0)` inside that excluded support
would invent ice inside sediment. The excluded cell volume is 0.521412 d³,
consistent with a discretized radius-0.5 sphere (exact 0.523599 d³). The diagnostic
C_S integral itself is 0.241822 d³ and must not be compared with sphere volume.
Full-mesh scalar integrals, however, retain support extension values as required
by the established conservation audit. The report and field integrals use these
respective conventions. All 401 independently integrated field ice volumes
match the same-time in-solver profile values to **5.33e−14 d³**. No moving-grain phantom-wake test is possible while the
grain remains locked. Weak diffuse ice tails in the water are not evidence of a
particle wake here.

## Decision and revised planning

The feasibility segment is complete; **the full P1 post-escape objective is
not achieved, and P1 is not accepted for production**. Conservation and cost are
now measured. The next technical work is to resolve the freeze/melt and tracer
interpretation, and assess the ice-rigidity criterion in the selected scaling,
before spending on a long continuation or P2. This analysis changes no physical
parameters and submits no additional simulation.

Applying the observed rate to the earlier one-diameter conduction scenario
would cost about **665,627 SU before contingency**, versus 71,516.5 remaining.
That is only an updated *planning scenario*: this run shows net freezing, so
there is no measured melt-out time to plug into a reliable forecast.
A matched short t≈10 P2 at twice the step count and 5.243 million cells would
cost roughly **866 SU**, or **1,126 SU with 30% contingency**, if throughput
transfers. Finer-grid solver cost can differ, and this would still be a startup
sensitivity check, not the required post-escape comparison.

The measured 85.67-minute segment plus 30% wall-time margin is **111.37 minutes**.
Thus a future *identical bounded segment* could plausibly fit the current
2-hour `debug` limit, subject to performance variability and a revised
checkpoint-stop timer. The completed job used `shared`; no queue change or
resubmission was made. This does not establish that a longer P1 or P2 fits debug.

For future long planning, full-field output during a locked-grain interval
needs a lower cadence or an explicitly designed event-aware policy: this
0.62-second physical segment alone generated 284.64 GiB. Do not delete existing
restart histories or relax falling-grain sampling as a side effect.
